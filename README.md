# Bio-Inspired Cardiac Sleeve

**Ziyang Shen | Mechanical Engineering | UNSW Sydney | 2026**

## Project Overview

This project investigates a soft robotic cardiac sleeve actuated by thermally driven twisted and coiled artificial muscles (TCAMs). The research explores how actuator contraction can be transmitted through a compliant silicone structure to compress a flexible chamber, establishing a mechanical proof of concept for external cardiac assistance.

The work combines actuator characterisation, mechanical design, nonlinear finite element analysis, silicone fabrication, and benchtop experimentation.

## Research Approach

- **Actuator characterisation:** Fabrication and testing of TCAM actuators to quantify their axial contraction under thermal actuation.
- **Mechanical design:** CAD development of the sleeve geometry and actuator arrangement to investigate deformation and force transfer.
- **Nonlinear finite element analysis:** Large-deformation modelling to compare structural responses and support interpretation of prototype behaviour.
- **Prototype fabrication:** Silicone moulding, actuator integration, and assembly of the soft robotic sleeve.
- **Benchtop testing:** Evaluation using a water-filled flexible chamber connected to a transparent tube, with water-column displacement recorded during actuation and cooling.

## Experimental Findings

| Measurement | Result |
| --- | --- |
| Maximum axial contraction of a standalone actuator | 12.4% |
| Maximum water-column rise during integrated prototype testing | 64 mm |
| Repeated trials per test condition | 3 |

Actuation produced water-column displacement consistent with compression of the flexible chamber. During cooling, the water level returned towards its initial position. Comparisons between prototype configurations, supported by qualitative FEA, informed evaluation of sleeve geometry and actuator integration.

## Research Direction

Further development focuses on actuator response under controlled loading, thermal management, cyclic performance, and testing under more representative cardiac loading conditions.

**Research areas:** Soft robotics, artificial muscles, compliant mechanisms, nonlinear mechanics, and biomedical engineering.
